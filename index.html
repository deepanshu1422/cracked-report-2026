India Hiring, 2026

9 in 10 employers no longer trust a Tier-2/3 degree as proof you can do the job. Here is exactly what they trust instead.

Gi Group Holding surveyed 1,329 people, 750 employers and 579 entry-level candidates, across 12 sectors and 8 Indian cities, for its 2026 report "From Degrees to Capability: Redefining India's Entry-Level Talent Landscape."

What actually gets you shortlisted

For Tier-2/3 graduates, 76% of shortlisting runs on practical proof, not a degree. Certifications, internships, shipped projects, real software fluency. Pure academic merit decides only 6% of shortlists.

The 90-day playbook

Stop collecting proof your college already gave you. Start collecting proof it never could.

  1. Pick one certification employers actually weight (private or EdTech certs are valued by 45% of employers, nearly 2x government-backed certs at 15%). Finish it in 30 days.
  2. Ship one real project, not a tutorial clone. 39% of employers shortlist on demonstrable practical skills like tools and certifications; a project you can walk through in an interview beats a CGPA line every time.
  3. Get any internship or structured training, even unpaid or short-term. 37% of shortlisting runs on workplace exposure, and this is the single biggest gap for Tier-2/3 candidates (over 80% report no access to industry-standard tools in college).
  4. Fix functional communication deliberately. 74% of Tier-3 candidates report struggling with the English standard employers expect. This is trainable in weeks, not years, and it is one of the two most sought-after attributes (52%) after learning agility (59%).
  5. Apply off-campus in parallel, not after your campus drive ends. Companies with no-tier policies (TCS, Infosys, Wipro, Cognizant, Capgemini) run skill-based filters that do not check your college name first.
